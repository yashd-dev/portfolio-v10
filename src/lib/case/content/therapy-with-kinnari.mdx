<Section id="overview" title="Overview">
	<Lead>
		Therapy With Kinnari is the online home of a Mumbai psychologist's practice, built to feel like
		the sessions themselves: unhurried and safe.
	</Lead>
	<P>
		The practice offers talk therapy and art therapy, rooted in trauma-informed, evidence-based
		approaches. The site covers services, workshops, articles, a community space, FAQs and booking.
	</P>
</Section>

<Section id="problem" title="The problem">
	<P>
		The practice offers sessions, workshops and resources, and all of it has to feel approachable
		online. A therapy site can easily read like a clinic: sterile, credential-first, and hard to
		take the first step from. For someone already overwhelmed, that tone is a wall.
	</P>
</Section>

<Section id="voice" title="A calmer voice">
	<Pullquote>For the days when everything feels like too much.</Pullquote>
	<P>
		The homepage leads with how the visitor feels, not with the therapist's qualifications. A Carl
		Rogers line, "the good life is a process, not a state of being", sets the frame, and Kinnari
		introduces herself with "you're welcome here, exactly as you are."
	</P>
	<P>
		That voice carries through the whole site: therapy is described as "understanding you, not
		fixing you", with talk and art as two doors into the same room.
	</P>
</Section>

<Section id="structure" title="The structure">
	<P>
		The pages map to the ways someone might arrive: Services for what sessions look like, Workshops
		for group formats, Articles and Community for people not ready to book, and an FAQ for the
		practical worries, cost, format, confidentiality, that usually go unasked.
	</P>
	<Fig
		src="/case/therapy-with-kinnari/section-1.png"
		alt="Therapy With Kinnari page section describing the practice"
		caption="Sections are written for the visitor's state of mind, not the business's org chart."
		wide
	/>
</Section>

<Section id="booking" title="Booking without friction">
	<P>
		Every page offers the same low-pressure door: Let's Talk. Booking a session is one calm step,
		not a form gauntlet, because the moment someone decides to reach out is the moment the site
		should get out of the way.
	</P>
</Section>

<Section id="cms" title="Editing it herself">
	<P>
		The site runs on Next.js with Sanity behind it, so articles, workshops and FAQs are Kinnari's to
		change. A therapist's site is a living thing: new groups, new writing, new answers. None of that
		should need a developer.
	</P>
</Section>

<Section id="shipped" title="What shipped">
	<P>
		A calming live site for sessions, workshops and resources, with a softer booking experience,
		editable end to end through Sanity.
	</P>
</Section>

<Section id="takeaway" title="Takeaway">
	<P>
		Design here is mostly tone. The palette, the spacing and the copy all answer the same question:
		does this make the first step smaller?
	</P>
</Section>
