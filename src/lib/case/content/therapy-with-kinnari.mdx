<Section id="overview" title="Overview">
	<Lead>
		Therapy With Kinnari is the online home of a Mumbai psychologist's practice, built to feel like
		the sessions themselves: unhurried and safe.
	</Lead>
	<P>
		The practice offers talk therapy and art therapy, rooted in trauma-informed, evidence-based
		approaches, for children, teenagers and adults in one-on-one sessions. The site covers services,
		workshops, articles, a community space, FAQs and booking.
	</P>

<Section id="problem" title="The problem">
	<P>
		The practice had a website, but it had effectively been lost. The domain had been let expire,
		requested changes were going nowhere, and the Google Business profile patients rely on to find
		and trust the practice was in a mess. Online, the practice was hard to find and harder to reach,
		and none of it was hers to fix: every change had to go through someone else.
	</P>
	<P>
		So the brief was really two briefs. First, restore the basics: the domain, the business
		presence, the ability to change things. Then build a site that actually feels like Kinnari, on a
		subject where the wrong tone is a wall. A therapy site can easily read like a clinic: sterile,
		credential-first, and hard to take the first step from. For someone already overwhelmed, that
		tone costs the practice the exact visitor it exists for.
	</P>
</Section>

<Section id="role" title="Role and constraints">
	<P>
		I took on the recovery and the rebuild end to end: restored the domain, sorted out the Google
		Business setup, then designed and built the new site and the CMS behind it.
	</P>
	<P>The design constraints shaped everything:</P>
	<ul>
		<li>
			Picture the visitor honestly: someone arriving sad, confused or unsure, on one of their harder
			days. The measure of every page is how quickly that person can reach out.
		</li>
		<li>
			The client is a practitioner, not a technical user. Once the site launched, changing it could
			not mean calling a developer.
		</li>
		<li>The subject is sensitive. Privacy is a design requirement, not a policy page.</li>
		<li>
			Warmth without haze. The site had to stay clean and uncluttered, with concrete answers about
			cost, format and confidentiality never more than a tap away.
		</li>
	</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="decisions" title="Key decisions">
	<Subhead>Design for the overwhelmed visitor</Subhead>
	<P>
		The design brief I worked to was a person, not a market: someone having a hard day, arriving
		with low energy and less certainty. Every page is judged by one question: how quickly can this
		person reach out from here? That ruled out clever navigation, dense pages and anything that
		makes a tired visitor work. It is also why the site is clean rather than busy: clutter asks for
		attention this visitor does not have, so each screen carries one idea and one next step.
	</P>
	<Subhead>Many touchpoints, each one low-pressure</Subhead>
	<P>
		People reach out in different ways, especially when reaching out is hard. So the site keeps
		several doors open at all times: the Let's Talk invitation on every page, a WhatsApp button that
		follows the scroll for the visitor who would rather type than talk, plain contact details for
		those who want a phone number or an email address, and a Schedule A Session path for the person
		who is ready. No door is locked behind a long form, and no scroll depth leaves the visitor
		without a way forward.
	</P>
	<Subhead>Structure by state of mind, not org chart</Subhead>
	<P>
		The pages map to the ways someone actually arrives: what sessions look like, what group formats
		exist, what to read if you are not ready to book, and the practical worries people rarely ask
		out loud. Nobody has to understand the practice to find their way around it.
	</P>
	<Subhead>Privacy in the details</Subhead>
	<P>
		Client kind words appear signed with flower names: Anemone, Marigold, Dahlia. The site can show
		what sessions mean to people without ever exposing who they are. For a therapy practice, that
		choice is the brand.
	</P>
</Section>

<Section id="voice" title="A calmer voice">
	<Pullquote>For the days when everything feels like too much.</Pullquote>
	<P>
		The homepage leads with how the visitor feels, not with the therapist's qualifications. A Carl
		Rogers line, "the good life is a process, not a state of being", sets the frame, and Kinnari
		introduces herself with "you're welcome here, exactly as you are."
	</P>
	<P>
		That voice carries through the whole site: therapy is described as "understanding you, not
		fixing you", with talk and art as two doors into the same room. Approaches like CBT, DBT,
		mindfulness and person-centered therapy are named plainly, as reassurance rather than jargon.
	</P>

<Section id="booking" title="Booking without friction">
	<P>
		Booking a session is one calm step, not a form gauntlet. Sessions work in person in Mulund West,
		Mumbai, or from the comfort of home, and the site says so in the visitor's own terms: "choose
		what feels right for you." The moment someone decides to reach out is the moment the site should
		get out of the way.
	</P>
</Section>

<Section id="outcome" title="Outcome">
	<P>
		The practice is back online on its own domain, with its Google Business presence restored and a
		site that finally feels like Kinnari. It runs on Next.js with Sanity behind it, so articles,
		workshops and FAQs are hers to change. A therapist's site is a living thing: new groups, new
		writing, new answers. None of that needs a developer anymore.
	</P>
	<P>
		The result is warm where the practice's old presence was broken and clinical, and structured
		around the visitor's first step.
	</P>
